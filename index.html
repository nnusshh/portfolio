The Work Between · TIAA

What happens after the Figma file handoff?

Role
Build Designer
Duration
2025–2026
Industry
Financial Services
Client
TIAA
Credits
Accenture Song

Across three releases, 98% of UX defects were caught and resolved before they ever reached production.

Most design work has a clean ending. You align, you hand off, you move on. I thought that was the job.

On TIAA, I sat on the other side of that handoff.

And I started looking more carefully. A screen could match the design exactly and still behave wrong. An error state could exist and say the wrong thing. A developer's "small question" could point to a decision that had never actually been made.

Matching Figma was only one part of the work.

So I built a way to evaluate built experiences. Not just: does it match?

A checklist. A way to document issues. A clear threshold for when a screen was ready to move forward.

  • But: does it behave as intended? Does it make sense in context?
  • What happens when things go wrong?

What looked like a checking process became a shared language between design and engineering.

I learned to read the questions developers were asking — and to tell the difference between what the design system already answered, what product logic made obvious, and what needed to be escalated with enough context to move fast.

Over four releases, I got better at making that distinction.

The screen showed me where the problem was. Understanding the product helped me understand what the problem actually was.

Then more designers joined. The process I'd built could no longer live only in my head. My work shifted — my role became about building shared judgment across the team.

  • Walking designers through the product
  • Reviewing their findings
  • Helping them understand not just what was an issue, but why

The goal wasn't for everyone to check screens exactly like I did. It was to build shared judgment.

I started by finding issues myself. Eventually, I was helping others develop the eye to find them too.

A good process isn't just something you follow. At some point, it should become something other people can use without you.

The Work Between · Writing

I've been thinking about this a lot lately.

Earlier, the design process naturally built a story.

Ideas led to decisions.

Decisions came with trade-offs. Trade-offs shaped conversations.

Each step carried reasoning forward.

But with AI, we are often jumping straight to the outcome.

But then — when the outcome arrives faster than the reasoning…

We suddenly have to
reconstruct the story.

When visible process disappears, Articulation becomes the only proof that thinking happened.

AI is definitely good at helping us start — but…

With the advent of AI

Making decisions!

Choosing what's right? Choosing which direction survives?

Rather than… spending time building the groundwork.

Selection Requires Judgment.

Judgment Requires Articulation.

It's not enough for a designer to make the right decision. The room also needs to understand the thinking behind it.

Vantage 360 Navigator

A secure, access-controlled platform giving each persona streamlined, personalised access to firm-wide FP&A reporting — one intelligent hub for every metric deployed to date.

01
Context

FIS is a global fintech across four segments — Banking Solutions, Capital Markets, CIO Organisation and Corporate — each with its own finance team, P&L and reporting rhythm.

In 2025 their reporting lived in a fragmented Power BI landscape: dashboards spread across locations, teams losing time hunting for the right report, the same views served to everyone regardless of need, and heavy dependence on FP&A just to get access.

The organisation needed a more intelligent solution — a way to translate those Power BI reports into agentic AI dashboards tailored to each market.

From opportunity to solution
OpportunitySolution
Fragmented reporting spread across many locations
Centralised into one enterprise hub
Time-consuming search for the right report or KPI
Clear visibility to the key financial KPIs
One view for everyone, regardless of need
Personas for secure, role-based access
Low self-service; heavy dependence on FP&A
A self-service navigator on Power BI + Fabric
02
Impact

The navigator centralised a fragmented reporting landscape into a single, persona-aware hub — with measurable business outcomes.

03

Three people, three relationships with the same data. Each opens the same navigator but uses it differently — different depth, frequency and questions.

After

Dashboard→Report→Agent card→Done
04
The Agents

The architectural shift: the AI runs on a schedule, not on demand. Four agents run every night after the ERP batch closes, so by 9am the analysis is already done. That's the difference between a dashboard with a chatbot and an agentic product — a chatbot waits to be asked; these agents do the work first.

Anomaly agent
Runs nightly · 3am
Checks every number against plan and forecast overnight, and flags only what actually moved.
Variance agent
When you ask
Explains what drove any gap — a region, a product or a team.
Freshness agent
Always on
Watches NetSuite, Workday and Salesforce, and warns when a number is stale.
Commentary agent
At month-end
Drafts the month-end commentary for you to edit and sign off.
05
The Screens

A walkthrough of the product — from launch, into the morning dashboard, and down into a persona's drill-down.

Product overview
One finance command centre — real-time metrics, AI-surfaced anomalies and forecasting in a single 360° view, so the morning's analysis is done before you sit down.
The morning dashboard. Five live KPIs, a ranked "needs attention" feed and a data-freshness check — the whole morning read in one glance, with Ask-Vantage on top for plain-English questions.
Persona drill-down with agent root-cause. Open any metric for actual-vs-plan, segment and regional variance — shown here as the CIO's cost-centre lens — with the agent's root cause, confidence and source alongside.
Metric reports
Revenue, OpEx, CapEx, Headcount, EBITDA — one screen each. Actual-vs-plan, an agent insight card (root cause + evidence + one action), and the forecast tab lives here.
Big Sheets
Dense hierarchical variance table. Agent flags on material rows, a cell-hover panel that explains any number, and a chat bound to the active filters.
Forecasting
A model-health screen, not a forecast viewer — accuracy, directional bias in plain English, macro driver weights, and a what-if panel that reruns the model.
CIO screen
Tailored to a cost-centre with no Revenue or EBITDA — CapEx, OpEx and Headcount, with platform variance by infra, cloud, and engineering.
Landing dashboard
Five KPIs, the ranked anomaly feed, a data-freshness panel, and chat with context already loaded — the whole morning read in one place.
Monthly close · in progress
The commentary editor — agent's overnight draft on the left, the analyst's editable version on the right. Submit locks it.

07
Reflections
TAKEAWAYS
Where to actually add the AI
The first build had a separate "AI Agents" tab that no one used — by the time you got there you'd left the data you wanted to ask about. The AI belongs inside the table, the chart and the chat: a layer on everything, not a place to visit.
Understanding the context comes first
This business segment was new to me and genuinely hard to understand. The time spent learning how each finance team actually worked shaped every design decision that followed.
Don't take anything at face value
Assumptions about what users "obviously" needed rarely survived contact with them. Every number, request and requirement was worth questioning before designing around it.

Carma

Shipped & live

Turning climate uncertainty into financial resilience for smallholder farmers.

Role
Product Designer
Domain
Agri-Insurance
Scope
B2B SaaS · Shipped
Year
2024–2025
Client
Agtuall
Credits
Studio Carbon
01
The Problem
Agtuall's goal

Design an MVP that helps financial institutions use climate data to create and manage parametric insurance for smallholder farmers.

The user's problem

Actuaries and data managers lacked a streamlined way to design insurance products, explore data and assess risk.

Constraints
The product architecture was still evolving alongside a nascent startup.
As an MVP, we had to prioritise the most critical workflows.
The design needed to support white-labelling and future customisation.
02
Demo

The main product walkthrough — exploring climate data, designing insurance products, and monitoring them end to end.

03
What Surprised Me · What Was Difficult

I initially saw this as a data-heavy SaaS problem, but research revealed that the bigger challenge was defining the product architecture and creating a clear workflow across data exploration, insurance design, monitoring and inspection. Aligning this structure took two weeks and 5–6 rounds of discussion, as both the startup and design team were shaping the product together.

04
Trade-offs

I prioritised the workflows that were most critical for the MVP — data exploration, product monitoring and inspection — while identifying capabilities like AI-powered analysis, onboarding and accessibility as future opportunities.

05
What I Built
I designed the core experience across three connected areas.
01 · Explore & analyse data
Users can search, filter, visualise and compare climate datasets to support insurance product decisions.
02 · Monitor overall performance
Insurance manager can easily overview the analytical data for all insurance products in target.
03 · Inspect underwritten dataset
Users can investigate a specific policy by comparing its underwritten weather dataset against similar datasets to better understand discrepancies.
06
Key Interaction Decisions
Search and filtering to reduce the effort of navigating large datasets.
Dataset comparison through visibility controls and multiple visualisations.
Drill-down navigation from Product → Insurance Unit → Policy.
Bulk dataset download workflow, drilled to the exact multi-level location.
07
Did It Work?
The MVP was successfully released and is currently being used by actuaries and insurance orgs.

We tested the prototype with 5 participants — 2 experienced actuaries and 3 beginner users. The sessions used task-based, moderated usability testing with a think-aloud approach.

Impact vs Effort matrix · PSSUQ usability score
08
Key Lesson
For complex expert tools, visualising information is only half the job.

The bigger design challenge is making relationships, comparisons and system logic obvious enough that users don't have to decode how the product works.

Unilever App- UX Audit

Enhancing Unilever's app to make everyday ordering simpler for retailers and sales reps in Vietnam.

Overview
Unilever · Vietnam

Unilever was enhancing its Vietnam retail app to create a more seamless ordering experience for retailers and sales representatives.

I analysed the existing interface across key touchpoints in the ordering journey and identified opportunities to improve usability, discovery, and ordering efficiency.

Impact

All six recommendations were accepted and implemented by the client. Because every finding traced back to competitive evidence and the audit framework's business-impact rationale, there was nothing to argue against.

Impact
How stakeholders responded
01
All 6 screens on the roadmap
Every screen type had recommendations picked up and prioritised for development.
02
Evidence beat opinion
Benchmarking against 3 competitors turned our findings into something stakeholders trusted and acted on.
03
Audit framework POC
The audit methodology I built became a repeatable process used beyond this project.
Constraints

The audit had to be built from the ground up — with almost nothing to start from.

No user research
No analytics to pull from
No UX audit framework — starting from scratch
Just the demo app to work from
Three competitors' data to benchmark against
Users

This audit evaluates the ordering experience for both retailers and Sales Reps using the app.

UX Audit framework
8 foundations · 40+ evaluation criteria
Experience analysis

Every core screen was benchmarked, then mapped to a foundation, a problem, and a concrete recommendation. Move through the screens below.

Homepage/Categories

Information Architecture & Navigation
Information Hierarchy & Structure
Promotions dominate the first fold, burying product discovery.
Add category listings above the fold to reduce clicks to purchase.

Search

Usability & Interaction
Search Experience
SKU-driven, with no room for exploration or brand discovery.
Introduce predictive search with trending brands and popular categories.

Categories

Information Architecture
Feature Grouping
Generic icons, no subcategories — browsing feels flat.
Replace with real product thumbnails and add subcategory depth.

Product listing page

Usability & Interaction
Task Flow Efficiency
Cramped layout, touch targets too small for mobile.
2-column grid, prominent filters, sorting options surfaced upfront.

Product description page

Information Architecture
Information Hierarchy & Structure

Surface final price, order limits, and promotions above the fold.
Learnings

Never be subjective.

Ground every UX observation in evidence, a clear rationale, and the user's task.

Good recommendations connect user needs to business goals — making design decisions easier to prioritise and act on.
